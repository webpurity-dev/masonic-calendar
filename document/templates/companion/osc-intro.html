Introduction to the Order of the Scarlet Cord

The Ancient and Masonic Order of the Scarlet Cord originated in Holland, using 18th-century documents from the Amsterdam Masonic archives. Introduced to England in 1889 by His Honour Judge Frederick Philbrick. Initially restricted to senior and distinguished Freemasons, the Order gradually declined and disappeared, surviving only in the archives of Mark Masons Hall.

Following the rediscovery of the archive and despite significant water damage to the ritual documents, the Order was successfully revived in 2006 by Right Worthy Brother Peter Glyn Williams, then Grand Supreme Ruler of the Order of the Secret Monitor. Its popularity grew rapidly, and in 2010 it became an independent Sovereign Order. Existing Conclaves were constituted as Consistories, organised into Provinces and Inspectorates.

The Order remains closely associated with the Order of the Secret Monitor, and membership is restricted to Princes of that Order. There are three initial Grades, corresponding broadly with the three Craft degrees.

On joining, a Companion receives the First Grade and becomes an Ostiarius, or Door-keeper. The Second Grade makes him a Lector, while the Third makes him a Fellow. An unusual feature is that advancement may take place in any Consistory, provided at least 28 days separate each ceremony.

The ceremonies are based upon biblical allegory, particularly incidents from the Old Testament. Although each ceremony initially appears to concern a separate biblical event, the individual themes are ultimately brought together in a dramatic and meaningful conclusion in the Third Grade.

The ceremonies involve active participation by a number of Consistory members, providing opportunities for experienced ritualists while also allowing less experienced Companions to contribute meaningfully.
